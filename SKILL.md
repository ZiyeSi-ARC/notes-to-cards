# Notes → Thinking Cards

Turn messy reading notes into a single, structured card with four cells:

**Quote → Idea → Example → Why it matters**

This keeps a note from being a passive highlight. The last two cells are where
the reader becomes a thinker.

## When to use

Use this skill when the user:
- pastes a paragraph from a book or article and wants to process it
- has scattered reading notes and wants them organised into cards
- wants to turn a book into a set of personal insights (for a course, essay,
  or their own archive)

## The card format

For each note, produce exactly these four cells:

| Cell | Question it answers |
|------|---------------------|
| **Quote** | What did the author actually say? (a short, verbatim line) |
| **Idea** | What is the author's point, in one sentence of your own words? |
| **Example** | What does this remind me of, today? (be specific — a video, a platform, a habit) |
| **Why it matters** | What does this change for me, or for the work I want to do? |

### Rules

- The **Example** cell must be concrete and specific. "A Bilibili edit of Obama
  framed as 'the most attractive president'" is good. "Modern media" is not.
- The **Why it matters** cell must be personal or actionable — not a summary of
  the book.
- If a note has no Example or no Why-it-matters, say so rather than inventing
  one. A note that only has Quote + Idea is a *quote*, not a *thinking card*.

**Keep it short.** Each cell should be 1–2 sentences. If a cell needs a paragraph,
you are cramming two ideas into one card — split it.

**The Idea cell is the hard one.** It is not a paraphrase of the quote. It is the
author's point, compressed into one sentence of your own words. Test: delete the
quote and the Idea should still stand on its own as a complete thought.

## How to use it

1. The user pastes an excerpt or a page of their notes.
2. You split it into one card per distinct idea (usually 1–4 cards per page).
3. You fill Quote and Idea yourself.
4. You **ask the user** for the Example and Why-it-matters when they are not in
   the pasted text — these should come from the user's own life, not yours.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **A vague Example.** "Modern media" is not an example — name the video, platform, or habit.
- **Idea is a paraphrase.** Restating the quote in different words is not understanding; compress the author's point instead.
- **Why it matters is a book report.** "This shows media is powerful" summarises the book; it does not say what changes for the reader.
- **Inventing a connection.** If the reader has no real Example or Why-it-matters, ask — never fabricate one.
- **Two ideas in one card.** A card that needs a paragraph to explain should be split.

## Checklist

- [ ] Quote is verbatim and short
- [ ] Idea is the author's point in the user's own words
- [ ] Example is a specific, contemporary, real thing
- [ ] Why it matters is personal, not a book summary
